Gatwick North or South Terminal? How It Changes Your Journey

Gatwick has two terminals, a railway station at only one of them and separate drop-off zones at each. Knowing which terminal you need shapes every part of the trip.

By {{AUTHOR_NAME}} · {{DATE_PUBLISHED}}

The short answer: check your terminal on your booking or the airline's website before you travel, for both the outbound and the return flight. The railway station is at the South Terminal, and a free shuttle links it to the North Terminal in a few minutes. If you are arriving by car, tell the driver your terminal in advance: each has its own drop-off zone, and the two are not next to each other.

London Gatwick sits about 30 miles south of central London, near Crawley in West Sussex. It is the UK's second-busiest airport and, unlike Heathrow, it has just two terminals. That sounds simple, and mostly it is. The trouble starts when passengers assume they can sort out the terminal on the day, or that their return flight lands where they left from.

Find your terminal before you plan anything else

Your terminal depends on the airline that operates your flight, not necessarily the one you booked with. On a codeshare ticket, the operating carrier decides. Terminal allocations also change from time to time, and some large airlines use both terminals for different routes.

  • Check your booking confirmation, e-ticket or airline app.
  • Check the airline's own Gatwick information, or the airport's flight search, shortly before you travel.
  • Check the return flight separately. A trip can easily leave from one terminal and come back to the other.

Write the terminal down alongside your flight number. You will need it for the train, for anyone dropping you off, and for any car you book to collect you.

How the two terminals connect

The North and South Terminals are linked by a free, automated shuttle train. The journey takes two to three minutes. Shuttles leave each terminal about every five minutes, dropping to about every ten minutes late at night and early in the morning. You do not need a ticket, but the shuttle runs airside of security, so allow time for walking, waiting and lifts.

Arriving by train

Gatwick's railway station is at the South Terminal, about five minutes' walk from check-in. If you are flying from the North Terminal, add the shuttle ride and a few minutes to your plan.

Three operators serve the station. The Gatwick Express runs to London Victoria twice an hour in about 30 minutes. Thameslink connects with London Bridge, Blackfriars, Farringdon and St Pancras, and continues north towards Bedford and Cambridge. Southern serves Victoria, Clapham Junction and much of Sussex and Surrey. That makes rail straightforward for most of London and the south-east, as long as your timing fits the timetable. Check National Rail for first and last trains and weekend engineering works on your date.

Being dropped off or collected by car

Each terminal has its own forecourt drop-off zone, and Gatwick charges vehicles to use them. Cameras read number plates, there are no payment machines on the forecourt, and the charge must be paid online or by phone by midnight the following day. The free alternative is the Long Stay car parks, where shuttle buses run to both terminals.

Collection works differently from drop-off. Drivers meeting arriving passengers generally wait in the terminal's Short Stay car park rather than on the forecourt. Because each terminal has its own car parks, a driver waiting at the wrong terminal means a delay while they move across.

That is why the terminal is the most useful detail to give when you book any car. With a pre-booked Gatwick Airport transfer, share the terminal for each leg along with your flight number, so the driver goes straight to the right drop-off zone and the right car park for your return. It is especially worth doing for early departures, families and groups, when sorting out a mix-up in the car park eats into time you do not have.

Registered Blue Badge holders can usually drop off without charge for a set time, but the vehicle generally needs to be registered with the airport in advance. Check the current rules on Gatwick's website before you travel.

Coming home: meeting your driver at the right terminal

The return leg is where terminal mix-ups most often happen. You have just landed, your phone may be on low battery, and you may be arriving at a different terminal from the one you left. A little preparation before the trip pays off here.

  • Before you fly out, check which terminal your return flight uses and pass it to whoever is collecting you.
  • Agree a specific meeting point, such as a named café, exit or car park level, rather than "arrivals".
  • Message your driver once you have your bags, not when the plane lands. That is when you are actually ready to leave.
  • If your flight is diverted or the terminal changes at short notice, tell your driver as soon as you know.

Getting to Gatwick from outside London

Gatwick serves much more than London. It sits just off the M23, close to Crawley, and its rail links run south to Brighton and across Sussex and Surrey. For travellers in those counties, or in Kent and Hampshire, the airport can be closer than any other London airport. The same terminal rules apply wherever you start: check the terminal, and if you are driving or being driven, head for that terminal's drop-off zone or car park rather than following general signs for the airport.

Self-connecting at Gatwick

Some travellers book two separate tickets and change planes at Gatwick, often to save money. If the flights use different terminals, the connection takes longer: you may need to pass through arrivals, collect and re-check bags, cross by shuttle and clear security again. Airlines do not usually protect a connection between separate tickets, so build in generous time. If something goes wrong, a delay on the first flight is your problem, not the second airline's.

Mistakes travellers make

  1. Assuming the return terminal matches the outbound one. It often does not.
  2. Going by the name on the ticket. On a codeshare, the operating airline decides the terminal.
  3. Not allowing for the shuttle. Train passengers flying from North need a few extra minutes.
  4. Forgetting the drop-off charge. Drivers who do not pay by the deadline can face a penalty notice.
  5. Telling a driver "Gatwick" and nothing else. The terminal, flight number and meeting point should all be shared before the day.

Final thoughts

Gatwick's two-terminal layout is easy to handle once you know which terminal you need. Check it for both flights, allow for the shuttle if you arrive by train, and pass the terminal to anyone driving you. Those three steps prevent most of the small delays that turn into a rushed check-in.

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if I'm flying from Gatwick North or South?

Check your booking confirmation or the operating airline's website. Allocations can change, and some airlines use both terminals, so confirm shortly before you travel.

How long does it take to get between the North and South Terminals?

The free shuttle takes two to three minutes and runs about every five minutes, less often late at night. Allow extra time for walking and waiting.

Which Gatwick terminal has the train station?

The South Terminal. North Terminal passengers take the free shuttle from there.

Is there free drop-off at Gatwick?

Drop-off on the terminal forecourts is charged. Free drop-off is available at the Long Stay car parks, with shuttle buses to both terminals.
